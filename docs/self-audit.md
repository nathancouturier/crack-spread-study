## 1. Series fetched successfully, stale or failed, with vintages and provisional ranges

19 series, 19 `ok`, 0 `stale`, 0 `failed`. I ran `python scripts/refresh.py --offline`
and read `data/manifest.json` directly rather than trusting the summary table.

Machine fetched, 15 series. Hand seeded, 4 (`anchors`, `sp_global_reference`,
`events`, `eia_refinery_fuel_2023`). Two are not committable and live under
`data/private/`: `ttf_daily` and `ei_refinery_capacity_annual`.

Vintages are present and specific on every entry. They are not timestamps
pretending to be provenance: `dgec_brent_monthly` carries the workbook filename
plus its `Last-Modified` header, `opec_rotterdam_products_monthly` carries the
archive issue index, `dgec_note_printed_weekly` carries the note date read out of
the document rather than off the filename. That last distinction is real and is
tested.

**Finding 1.1, serious. Provisional information is parsed from the DGEC notes and
then thrown away.** SPEC section 2 rule 5 says provisional is labelled and every
vintage kept. Only the three JODI series carry `provisional_from` in the manifest.
Every DGEC series carries `null`. But the note decoder *does* read the provisional
flag correctly. I decoded all ten preserved notes myself and got:

```
(2025, 11)  provisional=False   from wb_NPG-2025.12.19.pdf
(2025, 12)  provisional=True    from wb_NPG-2025.12.19.pdf
(2026,  2)  provisional=False   from wb_NPG-2026.03.20.pdf, wb_NPG-2026.03.27.pdf
(2026,  3)  provisional=True,True,False,False   (four notes, flag flips as it finalises)
(2026,  4)  provisional=True    from wb_NPG-2026.04.03.pdf, wb_NPG-2026.04.17.pdf
(2026,  8)  provisional=False   from NPG-2026.09.04.pdf
(2026,  9)  provisional=True    from NPG-2026.09.04.pdf
```

That is seven months of printed monthly product quotations, each with a correct
provisional flag, and in the March 2026 case an actual vintage history showing a
figure moving from provisional to final. None of it reaches a cache, the manifest,
or a test. It is decoded on every run and discarded. This is exactly the material
SPEC section 2 rule 5 asks to be kept, and the project already has it in memory.

**Finding 1.2, minor. `dgec_mbr_monthly` declares no provisional month, and the
argument for that is weaker than the manifest note makes it sound.** The note says
"This workbook publishes complete months only and its last is 2026-08, which is
already over." A month being over is not the same as its figure being final. The
spec itself says August 2026 stood at 38.05 provisional on 28 August and to take
the final figure from the September notes. The cache carries 38.0505047 for August
2026, which rounds to exactly the figure the spec calls provisional. The note of
4 September does print August as not provisional, which I verified by decoding it,
so the conclusion is probably right. The stated reason is not the reason.

**Finding 3.3, nit. `RECONSTRUCTION_ERROR` in `dgec_note.py` is a typed dict of

## 10. Anything I am quietly unsure about

This is the list the owner asked for. These are suspicions, not proofs, and each
one says what I could not establish.

1. **The reconstruction's error bar measures the wrong axis, and I think the
   phrase "out of sample" will be read to mean something it does not.** The
   0.17 to 0.44 $/t figure is a *leave one series out* number: the offset is
   fitted on three products' anchors and used to predict the fourth's. All eight
   anchors sit on the **last two weeks** of a 105 week chart. So it is out of
   sample across products and entirely in sample across time. It says nothing
   about the accuracy of week 1 of 105. The note pair overlap evidence, 0.30 mean
   and 1.68 worst, is the figure that does speak to time, and it is the one that
   deserves to be quoted next to the series. Trouble (c) quantifies why I think
   this matters.

2. **Pixel quantisation in the reconstructed series is invisible in the data.** I
   noticed repeated identical values at adjacent weeks, for example Eurosuper at
   719.1192666134755 on both 2024-09-20 and 2024-09-27, and the same value again
   on 2024-11-01. Those are almost certainly two different prices landing on the
   same pixel row, not two flat weeks. Nothing in the file distinguishes a genuine
   flat week from a quantisation collision. I cannot prove any single case is a
   collision without the underlying prices, which is the whole problem.

3. **I cannot verify the 2026-03-11 date for the IEA stock release.** SPEC section
   6.5 says "Pin the exact date from the source". `events.json` gives 2026-03-11
   and cites the March 2026 Oil Market Report landing page, which is a page rather
   than a statement of that date. Offline I have no way to check it. Three other
   events, the OPEC respecifications and the World Bank definition changes, cite
   generic landing pages rather than the document that establishes the date. Those
   facts are corroborated inside the parsed data itself, so I believe them, but
   the `source_url` field does not carry the evidence it appears to.

4. **The `0.98 to 1.02` weeks per point gate is a window around a measurement of
   ten documents.** Recon measured 0.9986 to 1.0009. The gate is roughly 20 times
   wider than the observed spread. That is defensible as headroom, but it means a
   note whose points were, say, 2 percent off weekly would pass, and 2 percent over
   105 weeks is two weeks of drift at the left end. I could not construct a real
   note that does this, so I cannot say whether it can happen.

5. **The left hand end of every chart is anchored by nothing, and the only
   evidence about it is the tick residual.** `GATES["max_tick_residual_usd_t"]` is
   2.0 against a worst observed 0.588. The code comment is candid that this is
   "the only evidence available about the left hand end, where no anchor sits". I
   agree with the comment and I think the 2.0 is generous given the observation.

6. **The six oldest weeks of the reconstruction are the least defended data in the
   project and I am not sure the site will say so loudly enough.** 2022-07-01 to
   2022-08-05 are simultaneously (a) covered by exactly one note, so
   `cross_checked` is False, and (b) at the far left of that note's chart, 105
   weeks from its nearest anchor. Both weaknesses at once. They are flagged
   individually in the data. They are not flagged as compounding.

7. **`refresh.py --offline` cannot see a value that changed inside its bounds.** I
   proved this, see trouble (d). The Python test suite catches it. I am unsure
   whether that will still be true for series the tests do not anchor, which is
   most of them: only the MBR and the Brent conversion have value level assertions.
   A one digit change in, say, `opec_rotterdam_products_monthly` would survive all
   four gate steps. I did not test that specific case.

8. **The five skipped tests will not un-skip themselves.** See trouble (f). The
   substance is genuinely unverifiable, which I confirmed independently. But the
   skip is unconditional and there is no watcher, so the day a July 2026 note is
   collected, nothing changes and nobody is told.

9. **I did not verify a single primary source document.** Every licence quotation,
   every page number, every "recon 02 section 5.4" reference was taken as given. I
   checked internal consistency and arithmetic. I did not open the DGEC methodology
   PDF to confirm it says 7.55 on page 4, and I did not open the EIA table to
   confirm 1,021,246. If any of those transcriptions is wrong, everything
   downstream of it is wrong and this audit would not have caught it. The one
   thing that reduces my worry is that the seed files carry `verified_line` fields
   quoting the raw text of the source line, which is a good habit.

10. **The repository has never been committed.** `git log` reports no commits and
    `git ls-files --cached` is empty. Every guarantee about what is and is not in
    the committed tree is therefore currently a statement about the working tree
    plus `.gitignore`. The enforcement is real and correct, see trouble (b), but
    it has not yet been exercised by an actual commit, and the first `git add -A`
    is the moment to re-run `validate-data.mjs`.

11. **The 8.33 question is open and I think the docs are right to leave it open,
    but I am uneasy that `config.py` reads more settled than `open-questions.md`
    section 12 does.** A reader who checks the constant in the place the spec says
    to check it gets a shorter and more confident story than the one the project
    actually holds.

# Trouble hunting

## (a) Every constant's citation against the number it supports

I read all 30 non trivial literals in `config.py` against their stated sources.

**`BBL_PER_T_GASOIL = 7.45` and `BBL_PER_T_GASOLINE = 8.33`.** The citations are
the ICE crack contract specifications and SPEC section 4.1, and both support the
numbers: these are the divisors in the ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil and Eurobob crack
contracts. The decision to hold 8.33 rather than fit a factor is right and is the
decision SPEC section 6.6 requires.

The **corroboration prose** is where it goes wrong, and it goes wrong in both
directions at once. `config.py` presents a six month sample as the available
overlap and gives a range of 7.42 to 8.15. I recomputed the full overlap from the
committed caches:

```
DGEC Eurosuper (reconstructed, monthly mean) over OPEC MOMR premium gasoline
n = 44 months, 2022-07 to 2026-02
implied bbl/t   min 6.937   max 8.333   mean 7.7185   median 7.8429

DGEC Gazole over OPEC MOMR gasoil
n = 44 months
implied bbl/t   min 7.308   max 7.819   mean 7.5285   median 7.5313
```

The 7.718 figure I was asked about is therefore **correct and reproducible**, and
it is the mean over 44 months, not the six `config.py` describes. All six values
`config.py` lists reproduce individually against my numbers (7.62/7.618,
7.42/7.423, 8.15/8.154, 8.12/8.116, 8.04/8.036, 7.90/7.904), so the sample is
accurate and the sentence describing it is not.

`docs/open-questions.md` section 12 already carries the correct 44 month table and
the correct conclusion, including the genuinely alarming consequence: applying 8.33
leaves the MOMR row 8.82 $/bbl above DGEC's Eurosuper on average, which is far
larger than any plausible RON 95 to RON 98 spread. That is the honest framing and
it is the one `config.py` should carry.

## (b) No adapter has hardcoded a market value it should have fetched

SPEC section 2 rule 2 is a browser rule and there is no browser code, so I took the
task's reframing: has any adapter typed a number the source should have supplied?

**No. I could not find one.** Three searches:

1. The AST sweep in point 3 above turned up no price, no rate and no level in any
   adapter. The floors (`MIN_ROWS`, `MIN_OBSERVATIONS`) are tripwires that make the
   adapter fail if the source shrinks, which is the opposite of a hardcoded value.

2. I grepped every adapter for `fillna`, `interpolate`, `ffill`, `bfill` and
   `reindex`. There are exactly two hits that touch values. `config.monthly_mean`

3. I checked for a fallback value on the error path. There is none: `Adapter.run`

The one place a value is computed rather than fetched is the reconstruction, and
that is declared with its own `method` field, its own series name, its own manifest

## (c) Trying to break the weekly reconstruction

This is the most dangerous thing in the repository and I spent most of my time
here. Four attacks.

### Corrupted notes: fails loudly, 40 out of 40

I fuzzed a real note, `NPG-2026.09.04.pdf`, with 300 random byte flips per trial
across 40 trials, decoding each one:

```
raised an exception   40
decoded identically    0
decoded differently    0
```

**Not one corrupted file produced a plausible wrong answer.** I also tried targeted
corruptions: truncation to 50 and 90 percent both raise `NoteDecodeError` naming the
missing quotation table title, an empty file and a non PDF both raise a pdfminer
exception. A smashed `%PDF` header and a single inert byte flip decoded to
bit identical output, which is correct behaviour rather than a failure, since
nothing meaningful was damaged.

### Three polylines instead of four: fails loudly

I removed one price polyline from the real page geometry and re-ran the full
decode:

```
NoteDecodeError: the chart yielded 3 polyline(s) of 105 points, expected exactly 4
of equal length. Lengths seen: [61, 105, 105, 105]
```

Adding a fifth polyline of equal length under a new colour also raises, naming 5.
The error message carries the measurement and the limit, which is what a loud
failure should do.

### Missing axis: fails loudly

```
every y tick label removed    NoteDecodeError: only 0 y axis tick label(s) parsed, the minimum is 4
only 2 y tick labels left     NoteDecodeError: only 0 y axis tick label(s) parsed, the minimum is 4
every x date label removed    NoteDecodeError: the value axis does not fit its own tick labels:
                              worst residual 253.981 $/t against a limit of 2.00
```

All three stop. **Finding c.1, nit:** the third one stops for the wrong stated
reason. Removing the x band chars also removes characters the y band picks up, so
the tool reports an axis fit failure rather than a missing x axis. It fails, which
is what matters, but the message would send a reader to the wrong place.

### The attack that works: a distortion that is small at the anchors

This is the real finding. The calibration fits **one additive offset per note** on
**eight anchors**, and all eight anchors are the last two weeks of a 105 week
chart. So the gates are blind to any error that is small at the right hand end and
grows toward the left.

I confirmed the benign half first. A pure translation of every curve point is
absorbed exactly, as it should be: moving all points down 2 or 10 points changed
the output by **0.00 $/t at both ends**, because the anchors move with everything
else and the fitted offset cancels it.

Then I tilted the chart, leaving the right end untouched and bending the left:

```
left end bent by    anchor worst gate    oldest week moved    newest week moved
1 point             0.391  (limit 1.50)         7.35 $/t             0.04 $/t
3 points            0.462  (limit 1.50)        22.05 $/t             0.11 $/t
10 points           0.711  (limit 1.50)        73.49 $/t             0.35 $/t
30 points           rejected, for an unrelated reason (x labels moved out of band)
```

**A distortion that moves the oldest week by 73 $/t, which is about 10 $/bbl on the
gasoil crack, passes every gate with the headline anchor residual reading 0.711
against a limit of 1.50.** The module's own docstring is candid that the left end
is unanchored, and the tick residual gate is described as "the only evidence
available about the left hand end". I am confirming that this is not a theoretical
concern: the gates cannot see it, and the declared 0.17 to 0.44 $/t error bar is
measured entirely at the anchored end and does not bound it.

**What does defend it**, and it is a real defence, is the cross check between
overlapping notes, because a given week sits at a different horizontal position in
each note that plots it. I measured the coverage:

```
weeks with 2 or more independent chart geometries   194 of 219
mean spread across geometries    0.61 to 0.64 $/t depending on product
worst spread                     1.673 $/t, on Brent, which is the series
                                 BRENT_IS_WORST already names as the weakest
```

So for 194 of 219 weeks there is genuine, independent, time domain evidence and it
is good: mean spread under 0.65 $/t. The exposure is the **25 weeks with one
geometry**, which I verified are 2022-07-01 to 2022-08-05 (six) and 2026-05-01 to
2026-09-04 (nineteen). The six oldest are the worst case, because they are both
uncorroborated and at the extreme unanchored left of the only note that draws them.

**Verdict on (c): it fails loudly against every corruption and every structural
break I could construct, which is a genuinely good result. It degrades quietly
against exactly one class of fault, a smooth distortion anchored at the right end,
and for 194 of 219 weeks the note overlap catches that anyway. The six weeks of
July and early August 2022 have neither defence.**

## (d) Failure paths: unplug a source, corrupt a cache

### Unplugging a source

I built a fake adapter in a sandboxed data directory and ran six failure modes
against a good cache already on disk:

```
mode      outcome              manifest status   cache
unplug    raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
empty     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
short     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
cents     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
zeros     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
dupes     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
```

Every one keeps the previous cache byte for byte, writes `status: failed` with the
error text in `note`, and re-raises so the caller exits non zero. That is SPEC
section 5.4 implemented correctly. The notes are specific, for example
`"1 value(s) in 'brent_usd_bbl' outside [5, 250], for example 9999"` rather than a
generic failure.

### Corrupting a committed cache, out of bounds

I set July 2026 MBR to 9999.0 in `data/cache/dgec_mbr_monthly.csv` and ran the
offline gate:

```
refresh.py --offline    FAILED, 1 series: dgec_mbr_monthly: the committed cache
                        does not validate: 1 value(s) in 'mbr_usd_bbl' outside
                        [-50, 150], for example 9999. The file was NOT changed.
                        exit 1
validate-data.mjs       exit 1
```

Caught, loudly, in both languages, with the file left alone.

### Corrupting a committed cache, inside bounds: **this is a gap**

I then set the same cell to 99.99, which is a 172 percent error on a published
anchor and comfortably inside `BOUNDS_MARGIN_USD_BBL`:

```
refresh.py --offline    19 of 19 series ok
                        data/manifest.json unchanged, byte identical
                        exit 0
validate-data.mjs       15 of 15 checks passed
                        exit 0
```

**Finding d.1, serious. Two of the four gate steps are blind to a silently altered

## (e) NaN become zero, or zero become NaN

I scanned every value column of every cache for zeros and negatives.

**The prices and volumes are clean.** No zero anywhere in any price, rate, margin,
crack or intake column across all 16 files. The only negatives are three months of
negative MBR in `dgec_mbr_monthly`, which are real: a gross refining margin can be
negative in a bad month, and the bound is set to allow it. The FRED blank day
convention is handled correctly and the manifest note is explicit that "A blank is
NaN in the cache, never a zero". I verified the 283 and 279 blanks are blanks, not
zeros, and that the row counts distinguish `rows` (published values) from
`file_rows` (lines).

**Finding e.1, serious in principle, harmless today. Three "agreement" columns
encode an undefined value as zero, and zero there reads as a positive claim of
agreement.**

```
dgec_note_reconstructed_weekly   spread_* columns   25 rows have a single
                                 geometry, and all four spread columns read 0.0
dgec_note_printed_weekly         max_disagreement_usd_t   16 of 18 rows have
                                 n_notes = 1, and all 18 read 0
```

A spread between one observation is not zero, it is undefined. The module knows
this: `DEGENERATE_PAIR` in `dgec_note.py` says, about a different case, "a spread
of zero between them is arithmetic, not agreement", and the `n_independent_geometries`
column exists precisely to make that visible. The same reasoning applies with more
force to a spread over one geometry, and there the file writes 0 rather than NaN.

Today nothing reads these columns so nothing is wrong. The moment a chart or a
table renders "spread: 0.00" next to a week, it will be saying the two notes agreed
when there was only ever one note.

**The counter example, and it is the right way to do it.** `opec_rotterdam_products_monthly`
handles the identical situation correctly: of 305 months, 2 have `n_issues = 1`, and
in exactly those 2 `max_disagreement_usd_bbl` is **NaN**, not zero. The 279 zeros
are genuine agreements between two or more issues. So the project already contains
the correct pattern, applied in one adapter and not the other two.

**Finding e.2, nit.** `data/private/ttf_daily.csv` has 469 rows with `volume = 0`,
all in the early history. Yahoo reports zero for a day with no reported volume as
readily as for a day with genuinely none. The column is unused, the file is private
and uncommitted, and the manifest already records that before 2018-03-14 the
settlement is repeated into all four OHLC fields, so this is noted rather than
raised.

## (f) Are the five skipped tests genuinely unverifiable, or quietly avoided?

**The substance is genuinely unverifiable. I confirmed it independently. The
mechanism is a quiet avoidance, and the stated reason is false.**

### The substance holds

I decoded all ten preserved notes myself and enumerated every monthly column any of
them prints:

```
(2025, 11)  (2025, 12)  (2026, 2)  (2026, 3)  (2026, 4)  (2026, 8)  (2026, 9)

July 2026 present?   No.
```

July 2026 monthly averages appear only in an August 2026 note. No August 2026 note
survives in the corpus. The ministry deletes each note when the next appears, which
`data/seed/anchors.json` records with a `url_status` of "404 on 2026-09-13" against
the exact URL SPEC section 5.6 gives. So the five values cannot be checked against
anything this repository can reach, and skipping rather than omitting them is the
right call. `docs/open-questions.md` section 27 states all of this correctly, and
`data/seed/anchors.json` carries each of the five with `"status": "unverified"` and
an accurate `status_note`. That is good practice and it is better than a skip alone.

### The mechanism does not

`tests/test_dgec_anchor.py:432` calls `pytest.skip(...)` **unconditionally**. It
never opens the file it names, never checks whether a July 2026 column exists, and
never checks whether a new note has been collected. The docstring says "When a note
carrying July 2026 is collected, this turns into an assertion". Nothing makes that
happen. The only function that could, `build_printed_monthly()`, has zero callers
(finding 8.1). So the day the evidence arrives, the five tests will still skip and
no one will be told.

### The stated reason is false

The skip message reads:

> "... `C:\...\data\cache\dgec_note_printed_weekly.csv` does not exist, so no
> series in this repository carries the Rotterdam quotations ..."

**That file exists.** It is committed, it is 18 rows, it is in the manifest, and it
carries the Rotterdam quotations for Eurosuper, Gazole, Fioul domestique, Jet,
Fioul lourd TBTS and Brent date. The test's docstring repeats the error: "No series
in this repository carries the Rotterdam product quotations yet. They exist only
inside the weekly note, whose adapter is a later batch." That adapter now exists
and its cache is committed.

The message was written before the note adapter was built and was not updated when
it was. The second half of the reason, that no reachable document carries a July
2026 monthly column, remains true and is the reason that actually holds.

Notably, `data/seed/anchors.json` gets this right: its `status_note` says
"dgec_note_printed_weekly holds the eighteen weeks the surviving notes print and
none of them is a July 2026 monthly column", which is precise and correct. So the
repository contains both the accurate statement and the inaccurate one, and the
inaccurate one is the one that prints on every test run.

## (b) The alignment guarantee, which SPEC.md section 4.2 asks to be structural

It is structural inside `crack()` and `crack_values()`, and conventional
everywhere else. Six routes, all run, none of which raised:

1. **The bare scalar functions.** `crack_from_usd_t` and `crack_from_usd_bbl`
   are in `__all__`, take three floats, and know nothing about dates.
   `crack_from_usd_t(1160.0, 7.45, 83.73)` cracks a weekly DGEC quotation
   against a monthly Brent and returns 71.97. `tests/test_units.py` itself uses
   this path.

2. **The window is a hand typed literal.** `engine.Quote(..., engine.MONTHLY)`
   on a value that is in fact a weekly average is accepted without a murmur. The
   Quote validates that the window is one of three strings, never that it is the
   right one.

3. **`MarginInputs` and `evaluate` carry no date and no window at all.** The
   cracks are a `Mapping[str, float]`. The whole margin, the whole Model view,
   and every breakeven run on numbers with no provenance attached.

4. **`decompose_official` takes bare floats too.** `crack_values()` is the
   function that enforces the cross crack rule and it is entirely optional. I
   handed `decompose_official` the `.value` of a monthly gasoil crack and a
   weekly gasoline crack and it attributed both without complaint. `series.
   latest_view` already bypasses `crack_values`, by reconstructing the cracks as
   `contributions[name] / DGEC_VOLUME_YIELDS[name]`.

5. **`crack_values` never fires on a single crack.** Which is the common case.

6. **`src/engine.js` accepts legs that have no window and no date.** This is the
   worst of the six and it is finding 5. `requireAligned` compares
   `product.window !== brent.window`, and `undefined !== undefined` is false, so:

   ```
   crack() on two objects with no window field: 71.97136465324384 window = undefined
   crack() on two objects with no window and no date: 71.97136465324384 date = undefined window = undefined
   ```

   `makeQuote` exists and validates, and nothing forces a caller to use it. The
   Python equivalent raises `AttributeError` on the same input. A Gate 4 module
   that reads a row out of a JSON artifact and hands it straight to `crack()`
   gets a number where the pipeline gets a refusal.

And the measurement that makes the shape of this clear. I instrumented
`require_aligned` and ran the whole pipeline, `opec_monthly_cracks`,
`dgec_weekly_cracks`, `decomposition_for_month` and `latest_view`:

```
require_aligned calls across the whole pipeline: 2882
calls where the two legs had different dates  : 0
calls where the two legs had different windows: 0
```

Not "zero misalignments found". Zero that could ever have been found. In every
call site in `series.py` both legs are built from the same local `day` variable
and the same hand typed window constant, so the guard compares a variable with
itself, 2882 times. The real misalignment risk, a bad `merge` or a wrong
resample, happens upstream of the Quote and is invisible to it.

to ASCII keys.

never notices:**

- `observedYields` and `normaliseYields` return

- `runYieldsCase` compares `yields`, `total` and `basis` and never

- `gasoil_key` is `"gasoil"` in all 250 margin cases. The parameter exists

## The sixteen findings

### 1, serious. The margin after gas appears to charge the barrel for gas twice

This is the one I would fix first and it is not a coding error. It is a question
nobody in this repository seems to have asked.

`series.margin_after_gas_monthly` computes
`mbr_usd_bbl - 0.21217 * gas_usd_mmbtu`. That is SPEC.md section 4.4's formula
applied to SPEC.md section 4.3 layer 1's series. But this project's own
description of what the MBR is says the MBR is already net of purchased natural
gas. Three places in the repository say so:

- `config.DGEC_MASS_YIELDS`: `"gaz_naturel": 0.010,  # input, purchased`, and
  the block comment above it, "Brent at 100 percent and natural gas at 1.0
  percent are INPUTS, not outputs ... less the 1.0 percent of purchased gas".
- `engine.replicate_mbr`'s docstring, quoting the method note: "from the product
  revenues subtract the cost of the Brent date FOB and of the CIF natural gas,
  the oil freight, and insurance and losses", implemented as
  `cost_usd_t = brent + gas + freight + 0.003 * (brent + freight)`.
- `config.DGEC_UNPUBLISHED_METHOD_INPUTS`:
  `"peg_nord_gas_day_ahead",  # the gas purchase price, EUR/MWh`.

So DGEC buys gas at 1.0 percent of the crude tonne and charges it inside the
MBR, and this study then charges another 0.21217 MMBtu per barrel against the
same barrel.

The repository carries no Btu per tonne for natural gas, so I cannot size the
overlap from committed constants. With an outside heating value of 49.3 MMBtu
per tonne, which I am supplying and labelling as mine:

```
implied DGEC gas intensity   0.010 t/t / 7.55 bbl/t * 49.3 MMBtu/t = 0.0653 MMBtu/bbl
this study's intensity                                              0.21217 MMBtu/bbl
overlap                                                             31 percent

charged twice, latest month 2026-08 (gas at 21.11 $/MMBtu)          1.38 $/bbl
charged twice, 2022-10      (gas at 39.02 $/MMBtu)                  2.55 $/bbl
worst month in the sample                                           4.57 $/bbl
```

The latest month's published margin after gas is 33.5716 $/bbl. If this is
right, about 1.38 of that is a cost subtracted twice, and the 2022 numbers the
history view exists to explain are out by more.

Nothing anywhere in `src/` or `docs/` mentions it. I grepped for "double count",
"already net", "net of gas" and "counted twice" and the only hits are about
JETKERO in the JODI yields.

I am not certain. It is possible DGEC's gas line covers only process gas and not
hydrogen feedstock, in which case the overlap is smaller, and it is possible the
owner considered this and decided the MBR's "marge brute" is gross of it. But
the decision is not written down anywhere, the label on the site will be "margin
after gas", and a refining analyst is going to ask this question in the first
thirty seconds. SPEC.md section 4.4 writes `margin_after_gas = margin_gross -
gas_cost` and SPEC.md section 4.3 layer 1 calls the MBR a gross margin, so the
spec itself may have walked into this. Gate 2 is where it gets raised.

### 3, serious. Two implementations of the margin after gas, in Python, and nothing compares them

`engine.evaluate` and `series.margin_after_gas_monthly` both compute

## Attack 1, leakage in the expanding window

**Claim under test.** The expanding window out of sample RMSE is genuinely out of
sample, 72 forecasts from a 60 month training window on a 132 month common
sample.

**Sample, confirmed.** `common_sample_dates` returns 132 months, 2015-04 to
2026-03, with no month gaps. The 294 row horse frame has no month gaps either,
which matters for the lag question below. `EXPANDING_MIN_TRAIN_MONTHS` is 60, so
origins run 60 to 131 and there are 72 forecasts, 2020-04 to 2026-03. Baseline
RMSEs, capacity dependent: A 6.647079, B 6.643055, C 6.620152, mean only
benchmark 7.902006.

**The poison test.** Every common sample month from index 65 onward, that is
2020-09 to 2026-03, 67 frame rows, had `utilisation_pct` and `log_intake_pct`
overwritten with values around 1e6 plus noise of scale 1e5. The common sample
came back as the same 132 dates. Every forecast made at an origin before month
65, that is origins 60 to 64, is **bit for bit identical** to the clean run for
all three horses, tested with `np.array_equal` on the raw bytes of the error
arrays, maximum absolute difference exactly 0.0. After the poison point the
errors differ by up to 1.93e6, so the poison did reach the arithmetic it was
supposed to reach. The forecast arithmetic is clean.

**Where every quantity is actually computed.** This is the part of the attack
that matters, since a leak can be real without moving a forecast.

| Quantity | Computed where | Leaks? |
|---|---|---|
| Coefficients | Training rows `0..t-1` only, `np.linalg.lstsq` | No. Proved bit for bit |
| Mean only benchmark | `y_train.mean()`, training rows only | No |
| Newey-West truncation lag | `newey_west_lag(132) = 4`, the **full** common sample | Full sample choice. Reaches only the in sample standard errors printed beside the race. No out of sample number uses it. `loss_differential` takes its own lag from 72 and gets 3 |
| Month dummies | Pure calendar arithmetic on `date.dt.month` | No. Nothing is estimated |
| Episode dummies | Dates fixed in `EPISODES` from `data/seed/events.json`, window fixed at `EPISODE_MONTHS = 12`. Included in the design if and only if `0.0 < mean < 1.0` on the **full** 132 months | Full sample **specification** choice, numerically inert. See below |
| Closure step dummies | Fallback model only. `capacity_step_years()` reads the whole 1965 to 2025 Energy Institute capacity series | Same shape as the episode dummies, same inertness |
| Standardisation, demeaning, scaling | **None anywhere.** `_build_response` builds raw columns and nothing else | No |
| Lag construction | `work[regressor].shift(k)` on the **full** frame, before the sample restriction | No. It is a backward shift and the frame has zero month gaps, so lag k is exactly calendar month t minus k. Doing it on the full frame is what stops a horse with a later start getting a shorter lag, which is the right choice |
| Which 132 months | Computed on the full frame by requiring the dependent and all three regressors' lags to be present | Full sample. Unavoidable for a common sample race |
| The dependent itself | See the finding below | **Yes** |

**Why the full sample dummy selection is inert, measured not assumed.** At origin
60, 2020-04, the columns `episode_2022` and `episode_2026` are exactly zero in
every training row. `lstsq` with `rcond=None` returns the minimum norm solution,
so their coefficients come back at 5e-16 and 4e-16. Dropping those columns from
the fit entirely changes the forecast by at most 4.3e-14, checked at origins 60,
65 and 72. So a design that "knows" in 2020 that there will be an episode in 2026
gains nothing from knowing it. The docstring's claim is true.

### Finding 1.1, serious. The dependent is built with a number that did not exist at the forecast date

`capacity_monthly` on the default `CAPACITY_STEP` basis maps the Energy Institute
figure for year Y onto all twelve months of year Y. `capacity_annual`'s own
docstring says the cache date is a 31 December stamp, "which is what the figure
means". So January 2025 utilisation is divided by a 31 December 2025 capacity,
published in mid 2026.

It is not small. Capacity falls from 6585.989 kb/d in 2024 to 6238.000 in 2025, a
5.3 percent step, and it lands entirely on 1 January 2025:

| Month | Intake kb/d | Capacity kb/d | Utilisation pct |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-11 | 5387.75 | 6585.99 | 81.81 |
| 2024-12 | 5428.01 | 6585.99 | 82.42 |
| 2025-01 | 5403.52 | **6238.00** | **86.62** |
| 2025-02 | 4944.30 | 6238.00 | 79.26 |

Intake barely moved from December to January, 5428.0 to 5403.5, and the forecast
target jumped 4.20 percentage points. On the 2024 denominator 2025-01 would be
82.05, so the construction alone shifts the target by 4.57 points against an out
of sample RMSE of 6.62 to 6.65.

Twelve of the 72 forecast targets, all of 2025, are built on a capacity number
that did not exist at the forecast date. Three more, 2026-01 to 2026-03, are
built on the 2025 figure held flat, which is the documented and flagged
assumption. Fifteen of 72 in total.

This does not leak the dependent into the fit, which the poison test settles. It
means the claim "out of sample" is a claim about the estimation and not about the
target. A reader who took the 72 forecasts as a simulation of what this model
would have said in real time would be wrong for 2025, because the 2025 answer
itself was not knowable in 2025. The fix is not this auditor's to make. The
sentence is: the estimation is genuinely out of sample, the dependent's
denominator is not, and the affected months are 2025-01 to 2026-03.

### Note 1.2, not a finding. The whole out of sample window is the crisis

All 72 forecasts fall in 2020-04 to 2026-03. `EXPANDING_MIN_TRAIN_MONTHS = 60`
against a sample starting 2015-04 puts the first forecast in the month European
lockdowns bottomed. Open question 37 already says this and says it well, and
`methodology.md` section 4.6 says shortening the window to gain quieter months
would itself be a choice made after seeing the result. Both are right. Recorded
here only because Attack 2 depends on it: the loss differentials inherit that
variance.

**Attack 1 verdict: the claim holds.** The 72 forecasts are genuinely out of
sample in the estimation, the lag construction is clean, there is no
standardisation, and the two full sample choices that exist, the Newey-West lag
and the dummy inclusion rule, are provably inert for every out of sample number.
The one real leak is in the target, not the fit.

## Attack 2, the power of the dead heat

**Claim under test.** No pairwise out of sample loss differential is
distinguishable from zero, t between 0.01 and 0.82 on 72 forecasts, so the race
is a dead heat.

**Reproduced.** t of +0.011, +0.045, +0.067 under the capacity dependent and
+0.359, +0.550, +0.817 under the fallback. That is the claimed range exactly.

**Is it the right test, on identical forecasts?** Yes to both.
`loss_differential` raises if the two horses were scored on different months, and
all three horses take their sample from the one `common_sample_dates` call, so
the 72 months are identical by construction and verified. The test is the
ordinary Diebold Mariano comparison, the paired squared error difference
regressed on a constant with a HAC standard error, which is the appropriate test
for **non nested** models, and A, B and C are non nested: same controls, one
different regressor each. It is robust to the lag choice, t staying between
+0.011 and +0.104 at Newey-West lags of 0, 1, 3 and 4. Using the normal critical
value rather than a t on 71 degrees of freedom makes rejection slightly easier,
so the failure to reject is not an artefact of the critical value either. The
test is correct and the conclusion "not distinguishable" is correct.

### Finding 2.1, serious. The race was never capable of detecting the gaps it was asked about

The minimum detectable mean squared error differential at 5 percent two sided is
1.96 times the measured Newey-West standard error. Converting that into an RMSE
gap against the better horse of each pair:

| Dependent | Pair | Observed RMSE gap | Smallest gap detectable | Ratio observed to detectable | Power against the observed effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| utilisation | A vs B | 0.061 pct | 9.18 pct | 0.006 | 0.050 |
| utilisation | A vs C | 0.405 pct | 14.15 pct | 0.023 | 0.050 |
| utilisation | B vs C | 0.345 pct | 8.81 pct | 0.034 | 0.051 |
| log intake | A vs B | 2.318 pct | 10.99 pct | 0.183 | 0.065 |
| log intake | A vs C | 3.823 pct | 11.92 pct | 0.281 | 0.085 |
| log intake | B vs C | 1.541 pct | 3.58 pct | 0.417 | 0.129 |

Read the last column first. The size of the test is 0.05. Five of the six
comparisons have power against their own observed effect of 0.050 to 0.085. A
test whose power equals its size is a coin that always says "not
distinguishable"; its failure to reject is not evidence about the null, it is
arithmetic about the sample.

The forecasts needed for 95 percent power at the observed effect size are 208,000
to 7.5 million under the capacity dependent and 1,400 to 7,300 under the
fallback. The kindest of the six, B against C on the fallback with the 1.54
percent gap the report quotes, needs about 1,400 monthly forecasts, roughly 117
years.

The reason is visible in the raw series. For B against C the mean squared error
differential is +0.3038 against a cross sectional standard deviation of 24.90 on
72 points. The five largest of those 72 months, 2022-01, 2022-02, 2023-10,
2021-12 and 2022-05, carry 35.6 percent of the total absolute loss differential.
The signal is a third of a unit and the noise is twenty five.

**Why this changes what the site says.** "Dead heat" asserts that the horses are
equal. The measurement supports only that this sample cannot separate them, and
would not have separated them had one horse been ten percent better. Those are
different claims and the second one is the true one.

Open question 35 already frames this as power and its closing recommendation, "a
reader who accepts that these three are indistinguishable on this evidence is the
finding. The site should say the second thing", is exactly right. But the
recommendation has not reached the two places a reader will actually meet:

- `docs/methodology.md` section 4.6 prints "So the race is a **dead heat**" in
  bold.
- `analysis.horse_race_winner` emits the literal string "**THE RACE IS A DEAD
  HEAT** and the ordering above is a ranking of numbers, not a finding". The
  second half of that sentence is right. The first half contradicts it.

Open question 35 also estimates the requirement as a standard error "roughly an
order of magnitude smaller than the measured one". The measured requirement is a
factor of 2.4 to 150 on the effect, which is 19 to 100,000 times the number of
forecasts. The order of magnitude figure is optimistic by a wide margin for four
of the six pairs and should be replaced by the table above, which is the number a
reader can check.

**Attack 2 verdict: the claim holds as arithmetic and fails as English.** The
test is right, the forecasts are identical, the t statistics are right. The race
was underpowered by a factor of between 2.4 and 150, and "the sample cannot tell
the horses apart" is the honest sentence.

## Attack 4, the threshold rests on one episode

**Claim under test.** 21 of the 22 months below the estimated threshold of 2.03
$/bbl are one unbroken stretch, 2020-07 to 2022-03.

**Reproduced exactly.** Threshold 2.0290 $/bbl, level 83.525, slope below +4.5772
with a standard error of 0.8743, n 135, 2015-04 to 2026-06. Bootstrap interval
2.029 to 11.279, width 9.25, block 20 months read off the dependent's own ACF.
The verdict is **unidentified**, reached on the edge rule rather than the width
rule: the interval reaches the top of the searchable range.

**The months below, recomputed.** 22 months. The contiguous runs are exactly two:
`2016-10` alone, and `2020-07` through `2022-03`, which is 21 months. So 21 of
22, 95 percent, and the claim is right. It is also computed rather than typed,
which `report()` does correctly and deserves saying.

**Grid sensitivity, run.** All four quantile grids return unidentified and the
point estimate is stable:

| Grid | Threshold | CI | Width | Identified |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 to 0.95 | 2.029 | 2.029 to 11.035 | 9.01 | No, edge |
| 0.02 to 0.98 | 2.080 | 1.830 to 12.330 | 10.50 | No, width |
| 0.00 to 1.00 | 2.257 | 2.007 to 12.507 | 10.50 | No, width |
| 0.10 to 0.90 | 2.020 | 2.020 to 9.520 | 7.50 | No, edge |

Neither the verdict nor the point turns on where the grid was cut. That is a
clean result and it strengthens the study rather than weakening it.

### Finding 4.1, blocker for anyone who would read a threshold off this. Removing the episode does not widen the estimate, it reverses it

Dropping 2020-07 to 2022-03 entirely, 21 frame rows, leaving the estimation sample
at 114 months, and rerunning the whole search and bootstrap:

| | With the episode | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | **2.029** $/bbl | **9.870** $/bbl |
| Slope below | **+4.5772** (se 0.8743) | **-0.4938** (se 0.2729) |
| Months below | 22 of 135 | **101 of 114** |
| Longest run below | 21 | 63 |
| Kink R2 | 0.3215 | **0.0560** |
| Straight line R2 on the same sample | 0.0352 | 0.0471 |
| Mean utilisation below | 74.13 pct | **83.94 pct** |
| Mean utilisation above | 83.57 pct | **81.16 pct** |
| Regressor range | -0.743 to 19.140 | 1.447 to 19.140 |
| Interval | 2.029 to 11.279, upper edge | 2.370 to 11.370, **both edges** |
| Verdict | unidentified | unidentified |

The slope **flips sign**. Without the episode, runs rise as the margin falls
below the estimated kink, which is the opposite of the hockey stick SPEC.md
section 6.2 describes. The "threshold" walks from near the bottom of the sample
to near the top, 101 of 114 months sit below it, and it is no longer a kink at
all: it buys 0.9 of a percentage point of R2 over a straight line, against 28.6
points with the episode in. The mean utilisation below and above swap places. And
the whole negative margin region of the regressor, everything from -0.743 up to
+1.447, is that one episode.

So the answer to this attack is the strongest available form. It is not that the
estimate becomes imprecise without 2020-07 to 2022-03. It is that the estimate,
its sign included, **is** 2020-07 to 2022-03. The unidentified verdict is correct
and, if anything, understated: an unidentified interval sounds like a wide
estimate of something real, and this is a description of one episode.

### Finding 4.2, serious. That check does not exist in the code, and this result is the least documented in Gate 3

`run_cut_threshold` has no `drop_episode_months` argument. `margin_response` and
`intake_trend_response` both do, and SPEC.md section 6.1's "show results with and
without the 2020, 2022 and 2026 episodes" is honoured for the response and not
for the threshold, which is the one result the site's headroom figure depends on.
Nothing selects on the omission, so this is not a section 6.6 problem. It is a
missing robustness check.

The documentation is thinner still:

- There is **no `methodology.md` section on the run cut threshold at all.**
  Section 4.6 refers the reader to "section 4.7 below" for it, and section 4.7 is
  "Endogeneity, which is not fixed and is not hidden". The cross reference is
  wrong and its target does not exist.
- The threshold appears in `methodology.md` only in section 7, one line saying it
  is unidentified.
- `open-questions.md` entries 35 to 43 cover the race, the out of sample window,
  the instrument, 2026, the two seasonal questions, the two dependents and cross
  checking. **None of them covers the threshold.**
- The 21 of 22 concentration, which is the single most important fact about this
  estimate, exists only in the text output of `analysis.report()` and in no

### Finding 4.3, minor but latent. `report()` prints the stretch dates from a positional guess

`report()` computes `longest_run_below` with a proper run length loop, but then
prints the stretch as `threshold.months_below[-longest]` to
`threshold.months_below[-1]`, which assumes the longest run sits at the **end**
of the list. Today the stray month is `2016-10` and sits first, so the printed
"The stretch runs 2020-07 to 2022-03" is correct by accident. Reversing the
arrangement, same 21 month run with a single stray month after it instead of
before, makes the same code print "The stretch runs **2020-08 to 2025-06**", a 59
month span that does not exist. Reproduced directly against the module's own loop.

The comment two lines above reads "MEASURED, NOT ASSERTED. Every clause of the
paragraph below is read off the result, because a sentence saying 'the kink is
one episode' that was typed rather than computed would go on saying it after the
next data refresh moved the months." The stretch dates are the one clause that is
not read off the result. `tests/test_analysis.py` asserts only that
`longest_run_below` is between 1 and `len(months_below)`, so no test covers it. A
JODI revision that pushes one later month below the threshold is enough to
trigger it.

**Attack 4 verdict: the claim holds and the consequence is larger than reported.**
21 of 22 is exact. Removing the stretch destroys the estimate and inverts its
sign, which is the strongest possible statement of why the verdict is
unidentified, and it belongs in the report.

### Finding 5.2, serious. The heating season window is a calendar year window, not a winter, and the published "16 of 25" is an artefact of it

November and December of year Y. Those are the tail of one winter and the head of
the next, two different winters.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calendar year Nov to Mar, **as published** | -0.365 | 1.045 | -0.35 | **16 of 25** | +0.360 |
| Contiguous winter Nov(Y) to Mar(Y+1) | -0.439 | 0.748 | -0.59 | **10 of 24** | -0.557 |

the finding SPEC.md section 6.5 asked to be checked rather than asserted is safe.

dragged down"; under the physically correct window it is positive in **10 of 24**,
a minority. The published count is a product of splitting winters at the year

### Finding 5.3, serious. The gasoil mean and median have opposite signs and the published sign rests on two years

Mean -0.365 against a median of +0.360, which is exactly why 16 of 25 years come
out positive under a negative mean. The per year differences make the reason
plain: 2022 at **-18.805** and 2008 at **-10.742**, with the next most negative
year at -6.392. Drop 2020 and 2022 and the mean flips to +0.265.

So the sign of the published gasoil number is a function of two observations out
of twenty five, and it is not stable: -0.37 on all years, +0.27 excluding the
crisis years, -0.44 on contiguous winters. The honest statement is that the gasoil
winter effect is **indistinguishable from zero under every definition tried**,
with t of -0.35, +0.35 and -0.59, and that quoting a signed -0.37 as the estimate
gives it more standing than the data supports. Under the contiguous winter the
mean and the median at least agree in sign, which is a further reason to prefer
that window.

**Attack 5 verdict: the numbers hold, two of the sentences do not.** The gasoline
result is solid. The gasoil textbook failure is solid and survives a stricter
test. The year count is right, the exclusions are arithmetically right and
verbally misleading, the weekly layer is not involved, and the October and
November strength is confirmed. The two sentences to change are "positive in only
16 of 25 years" and the presentation of -0.37 as a point estimate rather than as a
number whose sign flips on two observations.

   is still not available in January 2025 either way.
4. **The power calculation in Attack 2 treats the measured HAC standard error as
   if it were the true one**, which is what a post hoc minimum detectable effect
   is. It is the right statistic for "what could this test have detected" and it
   is not a designed power calculation, and I have not put a confidence interval
   on the standard error itself.
5. **The contiguous winter recomputation in Finding 5.2 is one defensible
   construction, not the only one.** I demeaned each calendar year by its own mean
   and then spliced. Demeaning by a winter centred year would give a different
   number. I report the one I ran, I did not search over alternatives, and I would
   not want the number I got treated as the definitive replacement.
6. **I did not audit horse A's separately reported long sample**, the 228
   forecasts from 2007-04. It sits outside the five attacks and it is the one
   place in the race where a genuinely varied out of sample period exists, so it
   is where I would look next.
7. **I did not examine `break_2026`, `endogeneity_diagnostic`,
   `imports_beside_intake`, `utilisation_annual`, `gasoil_long_sample` or the
   weekly seasonal range** beyond confirming they do not feed the five results
   above. Any of them could hold something.
8. **I read the 2SLS standard error rather than recomputing it.** The code builds
   the bread from the fitted regressors and the residuals from the actual ones,
   which is the correct HAC IV sandwich, and I checked the shape but did not
   verify it against a reference implementation. I let it go because the study
   declares the instrument unusable and nothing downstream reads it, which is a
   judgement about importance, not a check.
9. **The `0.0 < column.mean() < 1.0` inclusion rule is evaluated on the full
   common sample, and I proved it inert only for this data.** A dummy that is
   degenerate in the training rows without being exactly zero, for instance a
   closure step beginning inside the first training month, would not be inert, and
   the inertness argument in Attack 1 does not cover that case.
10. **Attacks 2 and 3 are findings about wording.** I am confident in the
    measurements. I am less confident that I have correctly judged how much they
    matter to a reader, and an owner who reads "dead heat" as shorthand for
    "indistinguishable" may reasonably think Finding 2.1 is a smaller thing than I
    have made it.
11. **I found no leakage in the expanding window fit and I want to say plainly
    that I looked for it and did not find it.** The poison test is the strongest
    check I know how to run and it came back clean to the bit. If leakage exists
    there, it is not of a kind that a poisoned dependent can reveal.

# Gate 3 self audit, what was done about it

Written after the audit above was acted on, by the agent that acted on it, and
kept in the same file so that neither half can be read without the other. The
audit above is left exactly as it was written. Nothing in it has been softened,
renumbered or deleted, because a record of what was found is worth more than a
tidy one.

All five findings were actioned. **One was a data defect and four were a right
number described wrongly**, which on a study whose whole value is honesty is not
the lesser category, and all five are closed below with the test that would catch
each of them coming back.

## Finding 1.1, the capacity look ahead. FIXED, and it moved the headline

`CAPACITY_SOURCE_LAG_YEARS = 1`. Month m of year Y is now divided by the Energy
Institute figure stamped 31 December of year Y-1, the capacity the region was
known to have when the month began, and `capacity_monthly` refuses to apply any
figure to a month before the date it describes.

Three consequences the auditor did not have to think about, all resolved out
loud in `docs/methodology.md` section 4.4:

1. **The closure step dummies carried the same defect and were moved with it.**
   `closure_step_2016` turned on at 2016-01 for a fall established only by the
   31 December 2016 stamp. It is now `closure_step_2017`, and `closure_step_2025`
   is now `closure_step_2026`. Unlike an episode dummy for an episode that has
   not happened yet, a closure dummy is NOT a column of zeros in the training
   rows of the expanding window, so this was a live look ahead in the out of
   sample exercise and not an inert one. It moved the fallback numbers.
2. **2026 stopped being an assumption.** The 31 December 2025 figure is the right
   denominator for every month of 2026, so the six months that had a numerator
   and no denominator now have both, no row is flagged `capacity_assumed`, and
   the basis argument was renamed `basis_beyond` because it is about the file
   running out and not about 2026. It still bites, and is still tested, for a
   month past the file's reach.
3. **The recon 03 anchor was preserved without being bent.** recon 03 section 2.3
   divided each year's intake by that same year's capacity, so
   `utilisation_sanity_check` now reports that column, still 11 of 11 to three
   decimals, AND the lagged column the regressions run on, AND the difference,
   which reaches -0.0439 in 2025. Neither can be substituted for the other.

The auditor's own uncertainty 3, that the finding rests on a reading of the
Energy Institute figure as year end, is unchanged and unverified against the
source. What the fix does not buy is a real time series: the reference date is no
longer in the future but the publication date still is, and that residual is now
`docs/open-questions.md` question 44 rather than nothing.

## Finding 2.1, the dead heat. FIXED as wording and as measurement

`analysis.horse_race_winner` no longer emits "THE RACE IS A DEAD HEAT". It emits
"THIS SAMPLE CANNOT TELL THE HORSES APART", says in the same sentence that this
is not the same as saying they are equal, and prints the power that says why.
`LossDifferential` now carries the observed RMSE gap, the smallest gap the test
could have detected, the power against the observed effect and the forecasts
needed for 95 percent power, all four measured. `docs/methodology.md` section 4.6
prints the table, `docs/open-questions.md` question 35 was reheaded and its "an
order of magnitude" estimate replaced by the measured factor, and a test fails if
the words "dead heat" appear anywhere in `analysis.report()`.

SPEC.md section 6.3 says that if A wins we say so. Under the corrected dependent
horse A now HAS the lowest out of sample RMSE under the capacity dependent, and
the report says so, reading it off the numbers as it always did. It is still not
distinguishable from the others and the report says that too.

## Finding 3.1, the instrument. FIXED, and it is a better result than it was

`gas_instrument` measures the first stage as the controls go in one at a time and
reports the instrument's own variation inside and outside the episode windows.
Both diagnoses the audit corrected are corrected: the first stage contains no
dependent and the two Fs differ only by the trend column, and the cancellation
argument stops at +0.10594 instead of being run all the way down to the reported
number. The report, `docs/methodology.md` section 4.7 and
`docs/open-questions.md` question 38 all now say that the instrument's variation
IS the 2022 shock and that SPEC.md section 6.1's equation removes it.

The equation was NOT re-run without the episode dummies to get a better F. That
would be choosing a specification after seeing what it does to a first stage,
which SPEC.md section 6.6 forbids.

One number differs from the audit's: the instrument's standard deviation inside
the episode windows is 18.61 $/MMBtu and outside 5.31 on the sample standard
deviation the module uses, against the audit's 18.29 and 5.29 on the population
one. The maximum, 60.16 in 2022-10, agrees exactly.

## Findings 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3, the threshold. FIXED

`run_cut_threshold` takes `drop_episode_months` and `drop_months`, so SPEC.md
section 6.1's "with and without the episodes" is honoured for the threshold as
well as for the response. The report and `docs/methodology.md` section 4.10,
which did not exist, both print the run that removes the one unbroken stretch the
estimate is drawn from. **The slope flips sign**, +3.8786 to -0.4958, the
threshold walks from 2.28 to 9.87 $/bbl and 101 of the 114 remaining months sit
below it. `docs/open-questions.md` question 45 is the entry the audit found
missing, and the wrong cross reference in `docs/methodology.md` section 4.6,
which pointed at endogeneity, now points at section 4.10.

Finding 4.3 is fixed at the source rather than in the printing: the longest run's
first and last month come out of the same loop that counts its length, and a test
with a stray month placed AFTER the run fails on the old positional guess.

## Findings 5.1, 5.2 and 5.3, the seasonality. FIXED

A season is now a set of (year offset, month) pairs, so the heating season can be
the contiguous November to March winter it physically is. The conclusion that the
textbook does not hold survives and stays, because SPEC.md section 6.5 asked for
it to be checked and not asserted, and it is nothing under every window tried.
The two sentences that supported it are gone: "positive in 16 of 25" is now
"positive in 10 of 24", the signed -0.37 is reported as a number whose sign rests
on two observations out of twenty five with its median of the opposite sign
beside it, and the exclusion row says it removes two complete years and not
three. Both windows are printed so the artefact is visible rather than quietly
corrected. The bare 2.0 in the `holds` flag, the one number the audit could not
trace, is now `Z95`.

## The section 6.6 inconsistency the audit raised in passing. FIXED

The module header and `docs/methodology.md` section 7 both said "no forecast"
while the horse race computed 432 one step ahead forecasts. Both now state the
resolution instead: SPEC.md section 6.3 asks for the expanding window by name,
that is the only kind of forecast in the project, none is shown as a prediction
of anything and nothing is optimised on them.
