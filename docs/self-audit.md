## 1. Series fetched successfully, stale or failed, with vintages and provisional ranges

19 series, 19 `ok`, 0 `stale`, 0 `failed`. I ran `python scripts/refresh.py --offline`
and read `data/manifest.json` directly rather than trusting the summary table.

Machine fetched, 15 series. Hand seeded, 4 (`anchors`, `sp_global_reference`,
`events`, `eia_refinery_fuel_2023`). Two are not committable and live under
`data/private/`: `ttf_daily` and `ei_refinery_capacity_annual`.

Vintages are present and specific on every entry. They are not timestamps
pretending to be provenance: `dgec_brent_monthly` carries the workbook filename
plus its `Last-Modified` header, `opec_rotterdam_products_monthly` carries the
archive issue index, `dgec_note_printed_weekly` carries the note date read out of
the document rather than off the filename. That last distinction is real and is
tested.

**Finding 1.1, serious. Provisional information is parsed from the DGEC notes and
then thrown away.** SPEC section 2 rule 5 says provisional is labelled and every
vintage kept. Only the three JODI series carry `provisional_from` in the manifest.
Every DGEC series carries `null`. But the note decoder *does* read the provisional
flag correctly. I decoded all ten preserved notes myself and got:

```
(2025, 11)  provisional=False   from wb_NPG-2025.12.19.pdf
(2025, 12)  provisional=True    from wb_NPG-2025.12.19.pdf
(2026,  2)  provisional=False   from wb_NPG-2026.03.20.pdf, wb_NPG-2026.03.27.pdf
(2026,  3)  provisional=True,True,False,False   (four notes, flag flips as it finalises)
(2026,  4)  provisional=True    from wb_NPG-2026.04.03.pdf, wb_NPG-2026.04.17.pdf
(2026,  8)  provisional=False   from NPG-2026.09.04.pdf
(2026,  9)  provisional=True    from NPG-2026.09.04.pdf
```

That is seven months of printed monthly product quotations, each with a correct
provisional flag, and in the March 2026 case an actual vintage history showing a
figure moving from provisional to final. None of it reaches a cache, the manifest,
or a test. It is decoded on every run and discarded. This is exactly the material
SPEC section 2 rule 5 asks to be kept, and the project already has it in memory.

**Finding 1.2, minor. `dgec_mbr_monthly` declares no provisional month, and the
argument for that is weaker than the manifest note makes it sound.** The note says
"This workbook publishes complete months only and its last is 2026-08, which is
already over." A month being over is not the same as its figure being final. The
spec itself says August 2026 stood at 38.05 provisional on 28 August and to take
the final figure from the September notes. The cache carries 38.0505047 for August
2026, which rounds to exactly the figure the spec calls provisional. The note of
4 September does print August as not provisional, which I verified by decoding it,
so the conclusion is probably right. The stated reason is not the reason.

The result is better than I expected. The overwhelming majority fall into four

Three things are worth naming.

**Finding 3.1, serious. The corroboration prose beside `BBL_PER_T_GASOLINE` and
`BBL_PER_T_GASOIL` contradicts the project's own best measurement.** The citations
themselves are fine, see trouble (a). The evidence written next to them is stale.

a subset presented as the whole. `docs/open-questions.md` section 12 already

The same applies to `BBL_PER_T_GASOIL`, where `config.py` says "7.31 to 7.65 across

This matters beyond tidiness because `config.py` is where the spec says the

**Finding 3.2, minor. `docs/open-questions.md` section 23 contradicts section 12

**Finding 3.3, nit. `RECONSTRUCTION_ERROR` in `dgec_note.py` is a typed dict of

0.17 to 0.44 band and the 0.87 worst case *are* genuinely recomputed per note at

## 10. Anything I am quietly unsure about

This is the list the owner asked for. These are suspicions, not proofs, and each
one says what I could not establish.

1. **The reconstruction's error bar measures the wrong axis, and I think the
   phrase "out of sample" will be read to mean something it does not.** The
   0.17 to 0.44 $/t figure is a *leave one series out* number: the offset is
   fitted on three products' anchors and used to predict the fourth's. All eight
   anchors sit on the **last two weeks** of a 105 week chart. So it is out of
   sample across products and entirely in sample across time. It says nothing
   about the accuracy of week 1 of 105. The note pair overlap evidence, 0.30 mean
   and 1.68 worst, is the figure that does speak to time, and it is the one that
   deserves to be quoted next to the series. Trouble (c) quantifies why I think
   this matters.

2. **Pixel quantisation in the reconstructed series is invisible in the data.** I
   noticed repeated identical values at adjacent weeks, for example Eurosuper at
   719.1192666134755 on both 2024-09-20 and 2024-09-27, and the same value again
   on 2024-11-01. Those are almost certainly two different prices landing on the
   same pixel row, not two flat weeks. Nothing in the file distinguishes a genuine
   flat week from a quantisation collision. I cannot prove any single case is a
   collision without the underlying prices, which is the whole problem.

3. **I cannot verify the 2026-03-11 date for the IEA stock release.** SPEC section
   6.5 says "Pin the exact date from the source". `events.json` gives 2026-03-11
   and cites the March 2026 Oil Market Report landing page, which is a page rather
   than a statement of that date. Offline I have no way to check it. Three other
   events, the OPEC respecifications and the World Bank definition changes, cite
   generic landing pages rather than the document that establishes the date. Those
   facts are corroborated inside the parsed data itself, so I believe them, but
   the `source_url` field does not carry the evidence it appears to.

4. **The `0.98 to 1.02` weeks per point gate is a window around a measurement of
   ten documents.** Recon measured 0.9986 to 1.0009. The gate is roughly 20 times
   wider than the observed spread. That is defensible as headroom, but it means a
   note whose points were, say, 2 percent off weekly would pass, and 2 percent over
   105 weeks is two weeks of drift at the left end. I could not construct a real
   note that does this, so I cannot say whether it can happen.

5. **The left hand end of every chart is anchored by nothing, and the only
   evidence about it is the tick residual.** `GATES["max_tick_residual_usd_t"]` is
   2.0 against a worst observed 0.588. The code comment is candid that this is
   "the only evidence available about the left hand end, where no anchor sits". I
   agree with the comment and I think the 2.0 is generous given the observation.

6. **The six oldest weeks of the reconstruction are the least defended data in the
   project and I am not sure the site will say so loudly enough.** 2022-07-01 to
   2022-08-05 are simultaneously (a) covered by exactly one note, so
   `cross_checked` is False, and (b) at the far left of that note's chart, 105
   weeks from its nearest anchor. Both weaknesses at once. They are flagged
   individually in the data. They are not flagged as compounding.

7. **`refresh.py --offline` cannot see a value that changed inside its bounds.** I
   proved this, see trouble (d). The Python test suite catches it. I am unsure
   whether that will still be true for series the tests do not anchor, which is
   most of them: only the MBR and the Brent conversion have value level assertions.
   A one digit change in, say, `opec_rotterdam_products_monthly` would survive all
   four gate steps. I did not test that specific case.

8. **The five skipped tests will not un-skip themselves.** See trouble (f). The
   substance is genuinely unverifiable, which I confirmed independently. But the
   skip is unconditional and there is no watcher, so the day a July 2026 note is
   collected, nothing changes and nobody is told.

9. **I did not verify a single primary source document.** Every licence quotation,
   every page number, every "recon 02 section 5.4" reference was taken as given. I
   checked internal consistency and arithmetic. I did not open the DGEC methodology
   PDF to confirm it says 7.55 on page 4, and I did not open the EIA table to
   confirm 1,021,246. If any of those transcriptions is wrong, everything
   downstream of it is wrong and this audit would not have caught it. The one
   thing that reduces my worry is that the seed files carry `verified_line` fields
   quoting the raw text of the source line, which is a good habit.

10. **The repository has never been committed.** `git log` reports no commits and
    `git ls-files --cached` is empty. Every guarantee about what is and is not in
    the committed tree is therefore currently a statement about the working tree
    plus `.gitignore`. The enforcement is real and correct, see trouble (b), but
    it has not yet been exercised by an actual commit, and the first `git add -A`
    is the moment to re-run `validate-data.mjs`.

11. **The 8.33 question is open and I think the docs are right to leave it open,
    but I am uneasy that `config.py` reads more settled than `open-questions.md`
    section 12 does.** A reader who checks the constant in the place the spec says
    to check it gets a shorter and more confident story than the one the project
    actually holds.

# Trouble hunting

## (a) Every constant's citation against the number it supports

I read all 30 non trivial literals in `config.py` against their stated sources.

**`BBL_PER_T_GASOIL = 7.45` and `BBL_PER_T_GASOLINE = 8.33`.** The citations are
the ICE crack contract specifications and SPEC section 4.1, and both support the
numbers: these are the divisors in the ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil and Eurobob crack
contracts. The decision to hold 8.33 rather than fit a factor is right and is the
decision SPEC section 6.6 requires.

The **corroboration prose** is where it goes wrong, and it goes wrong in both
directions at once. `config.py` presents a six month sample as the available
overlap and gives a range of 7.42 to 8.15. I recomputed the full overlap from the
committed caches:

```
DGEC Eurosuper (reconstructed, monthly mean) over OPEC MOMR premium gasoline
n = 44 months, 2022-07 to 2026-02
implied bbl/t   min 6.937   max 8.333   mean 7.7185   median 7.8429

DGEC Gazole over OPEC MOMR gasoil
n = 44 months
implied bbl/t   min 7.308   max 7.819   mean 7.5285   median 7.5313
```

The 7.718 figure I was asked about is therefore **correct and reproducible**, and
it is the mean over 44 months, not the six `config.py` describes. All six values
`config.py` lists reproduce individually against my numbers (7.62/7.618,
7.42/7.423, 8.15/8.154, 8.12/8.116, 8.04/8.036, 7.90/7.904), so the sample is
accurate and the sentence describing it is not.

`docs/open-questions.md` section 12 already carries the correct 44 month table and
the correct conclusion, including the genuinely alarming consequence: applying 8.33
leaves the MOMR row 8.82 $/bbl above DGEC's Eurosuper on average, which is far
larger than any plausible RON 95 to RON 98 spread. That is the honest framing and
it is the one `config.py` should carry.

Note also the maximum implied factor is **8.333**, in February 2025. Section 23 of
the same document says the factor is "never the ICE contract's 8.33". By the
project's own data it hits it.

**`BOUNDS_BRENT_USD_BBL = (5.0, 250.0)` against the spec's (10, 250).** The
justification checks out completely. I verified it against both Brent caches:

```
fred_brent_daily   25 published prices below 10 $/bbl
eia_brent_daily    25 published prices below 10 $/bbl
blocks             1998-11-30 to 1998-12-24, 17 days
                   1999-02-08 to 1999-02-18,  7 days, low 9.77
                   2020-04-21,                1 day
minimum            9.10 on 1998-12-10
```

Every element of the config comment reproduces: 25 prices, the three date blocks,
the 17 / 7 / 1 day counts, the 9.10 low on 1998-12-10, the 9.77 low in the 1999
block, and the claim that this is not a FRED artefact, since EIA's independent
workbook carries the identical 25. The reasoning is also right: a bound is a units
check, cutting 1998 and 2020 out of history to fit a printed bound would be closer
to inventing data than widening the bound is, and SPEC section 2 rule 1 outranks
section 5.4 here. The deviation is disclosed as a Gate 1 report item, which is what
the spec asks for.

**Finding a.1, nit.** The observed minimum is 9.10 and the bound moved to 5.0. A
bound of 9.0 would have cleared every real print with headroom and stayed much
closer to the spec. 5.0 is 45 percent below the evidence that justified moving it.
It still catches a cents file (910) and a decimal slip (0.91), so it functions, but
the widening is larger than the measurement requires and the comment does not say
why 5.0 rather than 9.

**Everything else in `config.py` checks out.** `MMBTU_PER_MWH = 3.412142` is the
exact ratio 3.6e9 / 1.05505585262e9 rounded to seven figures, which I confirmed.
`DGEC_BBL_PER_T_BRENT_NOTE = 7.5` reproduces the 628 $/t July 2026 anchor from the
published $/bbl, and the import time guard that raises if the two DGEC Brent
factors ever become equal is a good idea. The four EIA figures reproduce the
0.21217 intensity exactly from the committed seed. The bounds are all wide enough
to be units checks rather than market views, which is what they claim to be.

## (b) No adapter has hardcoded a market value it should have fetched

SPEC section 2 rule 2 is a browser rule and there is no browser code, so I took the
task's reframing: has any adapter typed a number the source should have supplied?

**No. I could not find one.** Three searches:

1. The AST sweep in point 3 above turned up no price, no rate and no level in any
   adapter. The floors (`MIN_ROWS`, `MIN_OBSERVATIONS`) are tripwires that make the
   adapter fail if the source shrinks, which is the opposite of a hardcoded value.

2. I grepped every adapter for `fillna`, `interpolate`, `ffill`, `bfill` and
   `reindex`. There are exactly two hits that touch values. `config.monthly_mean`
   reindexes a month span and `fillna(0)` on the **observation count**, not the
   value, which is correct: a month with no published days gets count 0 and value
   NaN. And `ei.py:491` calls `interpolate(method="linear", limit_area="inside")`,
   which SPEC section 6.1 explicitly asks for, which defaults to `how="step"` so
   the caller has to opt in, which refuses to extend past the last published year,
   and which writes to no cache.

3. I checked for a fallback value on the error path. There is none: `Adapter.run`
   re-raises on any failure and writes nothing.

The one place a value is computed rather than fetched is the reconstruction, and
that is declared with its own `method` field, its own series name, its own manifest
entry, its own licence note and its own measured error. That is the right handling
and it is better than the spec asks for.

## (c) Trying to break the weekly reconstruction

This is the most dangerous thing in the repository and I spent most of my time
here. Four attacks.

### Corrupted notes: fails loudly, 40 out of 40

I fuzzed a real note, `NPG-2026.09.04.pdf`, with 300 random byte flips per trial
across 40 trials, decoding each one:

```
raised an exception   40
decoded identically    0
decoded differently    0
```

**Not one corrupted file produced a plausible wrong answer.** I also tried targeted
corruptions: truncation to 50 and 90 percent both raise `NoteDecodeError` naming the
missing quotation table title, an empty file and a non PDF both raise a pdfminer
exception. A smashed `%PDF` header and a single inert byte flip decoded to
bit identical output, which is correct behaviour rather than a failure, since
nothing meaningful was damaged.

### Three polylines instead of four: fails loudly

I removed one price polyline from the real page geometry and re-ran the full
decode:

```
NoteDecodeError: the chart yielded 3 polyline(s) of 105 points, expected exactly 4
of equal length. Lengths seen: [61, 105, 105, 105]
```

Adding a fifth polyline of equal length under a new colour also raises, naming 5.
The error message carries the measurement and the limit, which is what a loud
failure should do.

### Missing axis: fails loudly

```
every y tick label removed    NoteDecodeError: only 0 y axis tick label(s) parsed, the minimum is 4
only 2 y tick labels left     NoteDecodeError: only 0 y axis tick label(s) parsed, the minimum is 4
every x date label removed    NoteDecodeError: the value axis does not fit its own tick labels:
                              worst residual 253.981 $/t against a limit of 2.00
```

All three stop. **Finding c.1, nit:** the third one stops for the wrong stated
reason. Removing the x band chars also removes characters the y band picks up, so
the tool reports an axis fit failure rather than a missing x axis. It fails, which
is what matters, but the message would send a reader to the wrong place.

### The attack that works: a distortion that is small at the anchors

This is the real finding. The calibration fits **one additive offset per note** on
**eight anchors**, and all eight anchors are the last two weeks of a 105 week
chart. So the gates are blind to any error that is small at the right hand end and
grows toward the left.

I confirmed the benign half first. A pure translation of every curve point is
absorbed exactly, as it should be: moving all points down 2 or 10 points changed
the output by **0.00 $/t at both ends**, because the anchors move with everything
else and the fitted offset cancels it.

Then I tilted the chart, leaving the right end untouched and bending the left:

```
left end bent by    anchor worst gate    oldest week moved    newest week moved
1 point             0.391  (limit 1.50)         7.35 $/t             0.04 $/t
3 points            0.462  (limit 1.50)        22.05 $/t             0.11 $/t
10 points           0.711  (limit 1.50)        73.49 $/t             0.35 $/t
30 points           rejected, for an unrelated reason (x labels moved out of band)
```

**A distortion that moves the oldest week by 73 $/t, which is about 10 $/bbl on the
gasoil crack, passes every gate with the headline anchor residual reading 0.711
against a limit of 1.50.** The module's own docstring is candid that the left end
is unanchored, and the tick residual gate is described as "the only evidence
available about the left hand end". I am confirming that this is not a theoretical
concern: the gates cannot see it, and the declared 0.17 to 0.44 $/t error bar is
measured entirely at the anchored end and does not bound it.

**What does defend it**, and it is a real defence, is the cross check between
overlapping notes, because a given week sits at a different horizontal position in
each note that plots it. I measured the coverage:

```
weeks with 2 or more independent chart geometries   194 of 219
mean spread across geometries    0.61 to 0.64 $/t depending on product
worst spread                     1.673 $/t, on Brent, which is the series
                                 BRENT_IS_WORST already names as the weakest
```

So for 194 of 219 weeks there is genuine, independent, time domain evidence and it
is good: mean spread under 0.65 $/t. The exposure is the **25 weeks with one
geometry**, which I verified are 2022-07-01 to 2022-08-05 (six) and 2026-05-01 to
2026-09-04 (nineteen). The six oldest are the worst case, because they are both
uncorroborated and at the extreme unanchored left of the only note that draws them.

**Verdict on (c): it fails loudly against every corruption and every structural
break I could construct, which is a genuinely good result. It degrades quietly
against exactly one class of fault, a smooth distortion anchored at the right end,
and for 194 of 219 weeks the note overlap catches that anyway. The six weeks of
July and early August 2022 have neither defence.**

## (d) Failure paths: unplug a source, corrupt a cache

### Unplugging a source

I built a fake adapter in a sandboxed data directory and ran six failure modes
against a good cache already on disk:

```
mode      outcome              manifest status   cache
unplug    raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
empty     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
short     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
cents     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
zeros     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
dupes     raised SourceError   failed            UNCHANGED
```

Every one keeps the previous cache byte for byte, writes `status: failed` with the
error text in `note`, and re-raises so the caller exits non zero. That is SPEC
section 5.4 implemented correctly. The notes are specific, for example
`"1 value(s) in 'brent_usd_bbl' outside [5, 250], for example 9999"` rather than a
generic failure.

### Corrupting a committed cache, out of bounds

I set July 2026 MBR to 9999.0 in `data/cache/dgec_mbr_monthly.csv` and ran the
offline gate:

```
refresh.py --offline    FAILED, 1 series: dgec_mbr_monthly: the committed cache
                        does not validate: 1 value(s) in 'mbr_usd_bbl' outside
                        [-50, 150], for example 9999. The file was NOT changed.
                        exit 1
validate-data.mjs       exit 1
```

Caught, loudly, in both languages, with the file left alone.

### Corrupting a committed cache, inside bounds: **this is a gap**

I then set the same cell to 99.99, which is a 172 percent error on a published
anchor and comfortably inside `BOUNDS_MARGIN_USD_BBL`:

```
refresh.py --offline    19 of 19 series ok
                        data/manifest.json unchanged, byte identical
                        exit 0
validate-data.mjs       15 of 15 checks passed
                        exit 0
pytest                  2 FAILED
                        test_dgec_anchor.py::test_published_mbr_anchor_parses_exactly[2026-07-01]
                        test_events_anchors.py::test_every_mbr_anchor_reproduces_from_the_committed_cache
```

**Finding d.1, serious. Two of the four gate steps are blind to a silently altered
value, and while it was altered the manifest continued to assert a reproduction
that was false.** `data/manifest.json` carries, on the `anchors` entry,
`"mbr_anchors_reproduced": 9` and a `note` beginning "9 of 9 published MBR anchors
reproduce exactly from the committed cache". The offline refresh does not re-derive
that field, so with a corrupted cache in place the manifest kept saying it while it
was untrue, and said so byte identically.

The test suite does catch it, and `make gate` runs the test suite, so the gate as a
whole holds. But the manifest is described in SPEC section 5.3 as a first class
artifact rendered on the provenance panel, and a measured claim inside it that is
not re-measured by the thing that rewrites it is a claim that can go stale without
anyone noticing.

I restored the file and confirmed `510 passed, 5 skipped` and a byte identical
manifest afterwards.

### Corrupting a private cache

I put an out of bounds value in `data/private/ttf_daily.csv`:

```
refresh.py --offline                   exit 1
                                       ttf_daily: PRESENT AND INVALID: 1 value(s)
                                       in 'close' outside [0, 500], for example 99999
                                       data/manifest.json unchanged, byte identical
refresh.py --offline --strict-private  exit 1, manifest entry set to failed
```

**Finding d.2, minor.** The default mode exits non zero, which is better than the
header of `refresh.py` led me to expect, so the gate does catch it. But it leaves
the manifest entry reading `"status": "ok"` for a cache the run has just declared
invalid. The committed manifest would then describe a file the tool knows is bad.
`--strict-private` fixes this and is not what `make gate` or the described CI runs.

## (e) NaN become zero, or zero become NaN

I scanned every value column of every cache for zeros and negatives.

**The prices and volumes are clean.** No zero anywhere in any price, rate, margin,
crack or intake column across all 16 files. The only negatives are three months of
negative MBR in `dgec_mbr_monthly`, which are real: a gross refining margin can be
negative in a bad month, and the bound is set to allow it. The FRED blank day
convention is handled correctly and the manifest note is explicit that "A blank is
NaN in the cache, never a zero". I verified the 283 and 279 blanks are blanks, not
zeros, and that the row counts distinguish `rows` (published values) from
`file_rows` (lines).

**Finding e.1, serious in principle, harmless today. Three "agreement" columns
encode an undefined value as zero, and zero there reads as a positive claim of
agreement.**

```
dgec_note_reconstructed_weekly   spread_* columns   25 rows have a single
                                 geometry, and all four spread columns read 0.0
dgec_note_printed_weekly         max_disagreement_usd_t   16 of 18 rows have
                                 n_notes = 1, and all 18 read 0
```

A spread between one observation is not zero, it is undefined. The module knows
this: `DEGENERATE_PAIR` in `dgec_note.py` says, about a different case, "a spread
of zero between them is arithmetic, not agreement", and the `n_independent_geometries`
column exists precisely to make that visible. The same reasoning applies with more
force to a spread over one geometry, and there the file writes 0 rather than NaN.

Today nothing reads these columns so nothing is wrong. The moment a chart or a
table renders "spread: 0.00" next to a week, it will be saying the two notes agreed
when there was only ever one note.

**The counter example, and it is the right way to do it.** `opec_rotterdam_products_monthly`
handles the identical situation correctly: of 305 months, 2 have `n_issues = 1`, and
in exactly those 2 `max_disagreement_usd_bbl` is **NaN**, not zero. The 279 zeros
are genuine agreements between two or more issues. So the project already contains
the correct pattern, applied in one adapter and not the other two.

**Finding e.2, nit.** `data/private/ttf_daily.csv` has 469 rows with `volume = 0`,
all in the early history. Yahoo reports zero for a day with no reported volume as
readily as for a day with genuinely none. The column is unused, the file is private
and uncommitted, and the manifest already records that before 2018-03-14 the
settlement is repeated into all four OHLC fields, so this is noted rather than
raised.

## (f) Are the five skipped tests genuinely unverifiable, or quietly avoided?

**The substance is genuinely unverifiable. I confirmed it independently. The
mechanism is a quiet avoidance, and the stated reason is false.**

### The substance holds

I decoded all ten preserved notes myself and enumerated every monthly column any of
them prints:

```
(2025, 11)  (2025, 12)  (2026, 2)  (2026, 3)  (2026, 4)  (2026, 8)  (2026, 9)

July 2026 present?   No.
```

July 2026 monthly averages appear only in an August 2026 note. No August 2026 note
survives in the corpus. The ministry deletes each note when the next appears, which
`data/seed/anchors.json` records with a `url_status` of "404 on 2026-09-13" against
the exact URL SPEC section 5.6 gives. So the five values cannot be checked against
anything this repository can reach, and skipping rather than omitting them is the
right call. `docs/open-questions.md` section 27 states all of this correctly, and
`data/seed/anchors.json` carries each of the five with `"status": "unverified"` and
an accurate `status_note`. That is good practice and it is better than a skip alone.

### The mechanism does not

`tests/test_dgec_anchor.py:432` calls `pytest.skip(...)` **unconditionally**. It
never opens the file it names, never checks whether a July 2026 column exists, and
never checks whether a new note has been collected. The docstring says "When a note
carrying July 2026 is collected, this turns into an assertion". Nothing makes that
happen. The only function that could, `build_printed_monthly()`, has zero callers
(finding 8.1). So the day the evidence arrives, the five tests will still skip and
no one will be told.

### The stated reason is false

The skip message reads:

> "... `C:\...\data\cache\dgec_note_printed_weekly.csv` does not exist, so no
> series in this repository carries the Rotterdam quotations ..."

**That file exists.** It is committed, it is 18 rows, it is in the manifest, and it
carries the Rotterdam quotations for Eurosuper, Gazole, Fioul domestique, Jet,
Fioul lourd TBTS and Brent date. The test's docstring repeats the error: "No series
in this repository carries the Rotterdam product quotations yet. They exist only
inside the weekly note, whose adapter is a later batch." That adapter now exists
and its cache is committed.

The message was written before the note adapter was built and was not updated when
it was. The second half of the reason, that no reachable document carries a July
2026 monthly column, remains true and is the reason that actually holds.

Notably, `data/seed/anchors.json` gets this right: its `status_note` says
"dgec_note_printed_weekly holds the eighteen weeks the surviving notes print and
none of them is a July 2026 monthly column", which is precise and correct. So the
repository contains both the accurate statement and the inaccurate one, and the
inaccurate one is the one that prints on every test run.

## Is this Gate 1 ready?

Yes, with the caveat that four things should be fixed before the owner signs it
rather than after. The data layer is the most carefully defended part of any
project I have audited: every failure path I could construct keeps the previous

it; the zero-for-undefined spread columns, before anything renders them; and
`config.py`'s stale evidence for the two conversion factors, because `config.py` is
where the spec sends a reader to check a constant.
